Pitting mechanism

Local breakdown of the passive film by chloride creates a small anodic region; metal dissolves while the large surrounding surface supports the cathodic reaction. Hydrolysis of dissolved metal ions acidifies the pit electrolyte and draws in more chloride, which can sustain propagation and reduce the effective wall thickness. Mechanisms differ with alloy and conditions, and increasing every parameter does not always increase corrosion proportionally.

Mathematical model (simplified, educational)

d(t) = d₀ + K · tⁿ (pit depth, mm; t in years)
K = K₀ · f_Cl · f_T · f_pH · f_O₂
dd/dt = n · K · t^(n−1) (modeled rate, mm/yr; t floored at 0.05 yr)
w_rem = max(0, w₀ − d)
f_Cl = (C/20000)^0.35 · f_T = exp[ E_a/R · (1/298.15 − 1/T) ] · f_O₂ = 0.2 + 0.8·√(c_O₂/8)
f_pH = 1 + 0.25(7−pH) if pH<7 · 1 if 7≤pH≤9 · 1/[1 + 0.4(pH−9)] if pH>9
QuantityTypeMeaning
C, T, pH, c_O₂, t, w₀, d₀User inputChloride (mg/L), temperature (°C), pH, dissolved O₂ (mg/L), exposure (yr), wall (mm), initial pit (mm)
n = 0.5AssumptionPower-law growth exponent; an empirical form common in pitting studies, value assumed here
E_a = 25 kJ/molAssumptionApparent activation energy for temperature scaling
K₀ = 0.5 mm·yr⁻ⁿCalibration factorSets the reference pit-growth scale at 20 000 mg/L Cl⁻, 25 °C, pH 7, 8 mg/L O₂. Illustrative only — not fitted to experiments
Exponents of f_Cl, f_O₂, f_pH slopesAssumptionMonotonic trends chosen for teaching
PipeAssumptionCarbon steel (API 5L X65-type), OD 508 mm
Limitations. No experimental validation is claimed. The model has no flow, scale, inhibitor, CO₂/H₂S, microbial, stress or repassivation effects, no pit-density statistics, and no temperature maximum from falling O₂ solubility. Use for comparison and teaching, not for integrity or fitness-for-service decisions.
